## Overview
I researched whether the Intuit Dome development could create measurable economic benefits for Inglewood residents and businesses. The project looked at employment, income, poverty, housing affordability, rent growth, and business activity.

The goal was not just to say whether stadium development is good or bad. I wanted to create a research framework that could actually test whether Inglewood's sports-led development strategy was producing measurable outcomes.

## Research Question
Can a major sports and entertainment development, combined with community benefits agreements, improve local economic outcomes without worsening affordability pressures?

## Project Flow
1. Researched Inglewood's economic development history and the Intuit Dome project.
2. Identified measurable outcomes: income, poverty, unemployment, rent, home values, and business activity.
3. Built a Difference-in-Differences research framework comparing Inglewood to similar cities such as Hawthorne and Compton.
4. Organized ACS, Zillow, and EDD labor market data into a supporting dataset.
5. Developed conclusions around both opportunity and risk.

## Key Findings
- Inglewood showed improving income and poverty trends, but the timing alone does not prove the Intuit Dome caused the improvement.
- Labor market recovery was meaningful after the pandemic, with EDD unemployment falling from 13.0% in 2020 to 5.6% in 2023.
- Housing affordability remained the biggest concern. Home values increased from about $600,000 to $715,000 in the Zillow sample, while rents also rose.
- The Intuit Dome and broader sports development may create jobs and business activity, but the benefits need to be compared against displacement and affordability pressure.

## Recommendation
The strongest recommendation was to evaluate the Intuit Dome using a Difference-in-Differences approach instead of only looking at before-and-after trends.

A strong evaluation should:
1. Compare Inglewood against similar nearby cities.
2. Track both economic growth and affordability pressure.
3. Measure whether local residents actually benefit from new jobs and business activity.
4. Monitor whether community benefits agreements translate into measurable housing and employment outcomes.

## Conclusion
My conclusion was that the Intuit Dome could be part of Inglewood's economic growth story, but the project should be judged by measurable resident outcomes, not just new development. A successful outcome would show job growth, rising income, stronger business activity, and improved housing stability without pushing out the residents the development was meant to benefit.
